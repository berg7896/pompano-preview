What is a wisdom tooth?

Wisdom teeth removals in Pompano Beach, FL

A wisdom tooth is a third molar, the last tooth at the back of each side of your jaw. They usually come in between ages 17 and 25. Most people have four, though some have fewer or none. Because they sit so far back, they often lack room to come in straight and are hard to keep clean, which is why they so often cause problems.

Dentist or oral surgeon?

A general dentist can handle a simple extraction. An oral surgeon is the right choice for complex cases:

  • Teeth close to nerves or the sinuses
  • Teeth deeply embedded in the bone
  • Patients who want or need general anesthesia

Our oral surgeon handles all of these here.

Why would I need a wisdom tooth removed?

Impaction

There isn't room, so the tooth stays trapped in the bone or grows in at an angle.

Crowding

It pushes neighboring teeth out of alignment.

Infection or gum disease

A partly erupted tooth leaves pockets where bacteria collect.

Tooth decay

The back of the mouth is hard to clean, so cavities form easily.

Cysts or tumors

Rare, but they can damage the jawbone and nearby teeth.

Pain and discomfort

Wisdom teeth can hurt even when nothing is visible.

Signs I need my wisdom tooth removed

If you notice any of these, call us. An X-ray will show what's going on.

Pain and tenderness

Pain behind your back molars can mean a tooth is coming in or impacted.

Swelling

A swollen jaw or inflamed gums can signal impaction or infection.

Bad breath or unpleasant taste

Often from bacteria around a partly erupted tooth.

Difficulty opening your mouth

Inflammation can stiffen the jaw.

Chewing issues

Pain when you chew may point to a wisdom tooth.

Cysts

A fluid-filled sac around the tooth needs treatment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my wisdom teeth are impacted?

Pain, swelling and trouble opening your mouth can all suggest impaction, but a dental X-ray is the reliable way to confirm it.

Will I be awake during the extraction procedure?

That's up to you and the complexity of the case. We offer local anesthesia, sedation or general anesthesia. See sedation dentistry.

What is the recovery time after extraction?

Most people rest for a few days and may have some swelling or soreness. Full recovery usually takes about two weeks.

Can I eat normally after the extraction?

Stick to soft foods and liquids for the first few days, and avoid spicy, hot or crunchy foods.

How long does the procedure usually take?

It depends on how many teeth and how complex they are. Removing all four takes about 45 minutes to an hour.
