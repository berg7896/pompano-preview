Signs it's time to have them out

Not every wisdom tooth needs to come out. At your consultation we take an X-ray and tell you honestly whether yours should.

Pain at the back of your jaw

Aching, pressure or headaches that come and go as the teeth push through.

Swollen or sore gums

Gum that keeps getting puffy or infected around a half-erupted tooth.

Impacted on an X-ray

Teeth stuck under the gum or growing in sideways against the next tooth.

Crowding

Your dentist or orthodontist wants them out to protect your bite.

Hard to keep clean

Food gets trapped back there, which leads to cavities and bad breath.

Before college or a big move

Many parents schedule it during a school break, before problems start.

What patients say

Questions people ask

Will it hurt?

You'll be numb, and with sedation most patients sleep through the procedure. Afterward, expect some soreness and swelling for a few days, which we help you manage.

Do I have to be put to sleep?

No. You can choose local anesthetic only, or sedation. Dr. Schaumberg goes over the options with you at your consultation.

How long is recovery?

Most people rest for a day or two and are back to school or work within a few days. You'll get written aftercare instructions and a number to call with questions.

Do you take insurance?

We accept most PPO dental plans and check your benefits for you before your procedure. We do not accept Medicaid. Financing is available on approved credit.

Can I book for my teenager?

Yes. Many of our wisdom teeth patients are teens. A parent or guardian comes to the consultation and signs consent for patients under 18.
