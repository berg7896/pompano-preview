What a dental implant is, and why it lasts

Dental implants can replace a single missing tooth, several teeth, or every tooth in the upper and lower arches. The titanium post fuses directly with your jawbone, so the new tooth is anchored like a natural root. That gives you the stability and chewing strength of a real tooth, which a bridge or a removable denture can't match.

Because the implant stimulates the bone the way a tooth root does, it also helps preserve your jawbone and the shape of your face over time.

Benefits of dental implants

  • A permanent restoration, not a removable one
  • Natural-looking crowns, made to match your teeth
  • Durable and long-lasting
  • Helps preserve your jawbone
  • Restores full chewing and speaking
  • A complete smile you can show with confidence

Dental implant questions

What are dental implants?

An implant is an artificial tooth root, usually titanium, placed in the jawbone to hold a crown, bridge or full arch of teeth. Once healed, it works like a natural tooth.

How do dental implants work?

The post is placed in the bone and fuses with it over a few months. An abutment then connects the post to a custom crown made in our lab.

What are dental implants made of?

Most implants are titanium, which bonds well with bone. Zirconia implants are an option for some patients; ask about them at your consult.

What are the benefits of dental implants?

They are permanent, look natural, restore full chewing, and help keep the jawbone from shrinking, unlike bridges or removable dentures.

How do zygomatic implants work?

When the upper jaw has too little bone, longer zygomatic implants anchor in the cheekbone instead, often avoiding bone grafting. Our oral surgeon will tell you if they suit you.

Does insurance cover dental implants?

Many PPO plans cover part of the cost, and we check your benefits for you. Without insurance, $1,789 is already the complete price, and financing is available.
